No waiting, no self
Zhuangzi's second sentence contains three words, called "Xiaoyaoyou", which is the title of one of his famous chapters. How many times have I copied "Xiaoyaoyou"? Why did I copy it? Because it is both my artistic ideal and my life ideal. I remember that in a commentary on CCTV, I once said that the three words "Happy Wandering" are the highest ideal of Chinese art and Chinese life.
The term "free and easy travel" seems very popular at first glance. We want to be free and easy. We want to wander in the sky thousands of miles away and in the vast world. How happy it is to be like this. But in fact, according to Zhuangzi, to truly achieve a free and easy journey, there are two nothings. One is called no waiting, we don’t wait for anything; the second is selfless, we don’t have much pursuit of ourselves.
What is no wait? Why not wait? Any waiting is actually already in a passive state, already in a controlled state. For example, Zhuangzi once said that there is a kind of big roc that can fly for half a month with strong winds in the nine heavens. This makes people feel very powerful. But Zhuangzi raised a question, you have to rely on strong winds to fly for half a month. Although spectacular, you are already bound by waiting. You can't fly without wind. If the wind turns, you have to turn, if the wind slows down, you have to slow down, and if the wind stops, you have to stop. Of course, in addition to wind, flying also depends on many other conditions, such as region, season, etc. So, what is the freedom of flight that people expect?
What I mean by "waiting" is actually a control, a control of control. As long as there are too many expectations, you will be too controlled; as a result, you will not be able to truly fly freely. In Zhuangzi's view, Xiaoyaoyou means getting rid of some expectations from the heart, getting rid of too many expectations and waiting. This is the same as Buddhist thinking. As I said before, Buddhism believes that because this thing is empty, you cannot expect too much. If you do, you will become a pursuer full of pain. In this case, pain will always follow you behind. Therefore, if we don’t have expectations and don’t have too much dependence, this “waiting” also includes the meaning of “waiting” including dependence. If we don’t have too much dependence, or even if we don’t have any more dependence, then you can have a free time. This is a case of no waiting.
In addition, Wuji, don't be too self-centered, don't always think about yourself, how am I, how am I, how am I? If you think like this, you can't be free. Xiaoyao was trapped by the word "I" and his own character "Ji". How can you be free? I am flying in the sky, and other birds fly faster than me, or fly lower than me, or wait, they are more swarming, there are more of them than me, etc. After thinking about it, your whole flight is not free. If you finally become the first person in the sky, you will worry about whether the second place will soon catch up with me, and you will worry about whether the fifth and sixth places will play tricks and cause me to get hurt, etc., etc. As long as there is heaven, expectations, and too much self, you will not be able to fly freely. Therefore, only carefree thinking is the thinking of Zhuangzi’s ideal life.
Taoist ideal of personality
This means that as long as a person can be free of others and selfless, he can get close to the real person and truly become the real person.
I once said that the goal of Confucianism is the gentleman, the goal of Buddhism is the enlightened person, and the goal of Taoism is the true person. These are their different personality ideals. Although the concept of real people was also proposed by celebrities in the Wei and Jin Dynasties, I think it was Zhuangzi who put forward this concept most completely early and provided the origin for celebrities in the Wei and Jin Dynasties. Zhuangzi said seriously what the real person is and what the real person is.
His original text is very eloquent and not too complicated. He said in "The Great Master": "What is a true person? A true person in ancient times does not rebel against the few, is not powerful, and is not immoral. If so, he will not regret it after passing, and he will not be complacent; if so, he will not tremble when climbing high, not wet when entering water, and not hot when entering fire. This is also the case for those who know how to ascend the false path and follow the Tao."
This sounds a bit awkward, so I will translate it into contemporary sentences. This is my specialty. I always try to translate ancient texts as much as possible. I will translate this paragraph of Zhuangzi’s discussion about real people, real people in ancient times, and I will read it to you. My translation is relatively accurate, and every word and sentence is translated according to strict academic requirements, so everyone can listen with confidence. 
This is how I translated it. This is what Zhuangzi said. I put it in modern terms. He said:
What is a real person? The real people in ancient times did not bully the weak, do not assert themselves, and did not plot. Such a person does not feel regretful when he loses something, and becomes unsatisfied; such a person does not tremble when he climbs a height, does not sink when he falls into the water, and does not feel the heat when he enters the fire. Only those whose knowledge is consistent with the Tao can do this.
The real people in ancient times slept without dreams, woke up without disturbance, did not seek sweet food and drink, and breathed deeply and thoroughly. The breath of a real person can penetrate through the heels, but the breath of ordinary people is only in the throat. Speech will be hindered if one gives in to others. Then, if you give in to your desires, your natural faculties will be shallow. The real people in ancient times were not greedy for life and did not hate death. There is no joy in being born; there is no rejection in death. Feel free to go as you feel free to come. Don’t forget the starting point and don’t pursue the end point. Accept it happily when it comes, and go back to nature if you forget it. This means that one can be called a true person only if he does not use the human heart to harm the Tao and does not use manpower to add to nature.
