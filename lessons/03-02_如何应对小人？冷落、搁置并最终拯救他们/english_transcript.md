# How to Deal with Xiaoren: Leave Them in the Cold, Put Them Aside, and Ultimately Redeem Them

Hello everyone, I am Yu Qiuyu, and let us continue our lecture. Earlier, I discussed why Xiaoren (petty persons) are so rampant and why we often feel helpless against them. There are five internal reasons on our part that make us utterly powerless when confronting them. These five reasons ultimately boil down to a single cause: traces of the Xiaoren still remain within ourselves, and we have not yet truly become Junzi (exemplary persons). Consequently, we suffer from their torment and succumb to their intimidation. Because we haven't thoroughly differentiated ourselves from them, it is impossible for us to decisively refute them. This was the core content of our previous discussion.

Continuously Expanding the Ranks of Junzi Through Self-Cultivation

On this issue, there are not many things we can do. The very first thing we can do is to continuously expand the ranks of Junzi, thereby shifting the numerical balance between Junzi and Xiaoren. Through our own self-cultivation, we must persistently transform ourselves into genuine, pure Junzi, while inspiring friends around us to become Junzi as well—this requires our concerted effort.

It is simply impossible to expect a Junzi to refute a Xiaoren using their own twisted logic. We can only rely on our own conduct to nurture society's reliance on and appreciation of justice, gradually bringing into stark relief the sheer ugliness of the Xiaoren. As long as genuine Junzi exist in society, people will realize that a far superior personality model and a more beautiful realm of life exist, and the market for Xiaoren will steadily shrink.

The ancient criteria for the Junzi represent their foundational distinction from the Xiaoren: for example, the Junzi cherishes virtue; the Junzi is serene and open-hearted; the Junzi seeks within himself; the Junzi brings out the best in others; the Junzi seeks harmony without uniformity; the Junzi is inclusive without colluding; the Junzi abides by the Golden Mean; the Junzi observes propriety; the Junzi is not a mere utensil; and the Junzi knows shame. If we conscientiously put these nine essentials into practice and truly live them, we will stand as dignified, upright Junzi.

The lived experience of a true Junzi is an exceptionally wonderful life journey; once you commit yourself to it wholeheartedly, you will swiftly realize that you have nothing left to fear. Previously, when you hesitated to become a Junzi, wasn't it precisely out of fear of Xiaoren? Once you have genuinely become a Junzi, such anxieties vanish altogether.

Everyone has mentioned that we are always afraid of Xiaoren stirring up trouble. But frankly, those who are genuinely afraid are the ones trapped halfway between a Junzi and a Xiaoren. If you truly become a great Junzi—like what Buddhism calls 'Mahāvīra' (The Great Hero)—you fear nothing; why would you still fear Xiaoren? To no longer fear anything we once dreaded—that is the inner conviction of a true Junzi: no longer fearing public slander that melts gold, no longer fearing being smeared by foul stench, no longer fearing swarms of flies, no longer fearing dark subterranean trenches, no longer fearing those famished gazes ever longing for new witch hunts; fearing neither eavesdropping, nor intimidation, nor sinister sneers. Only by demonstrating the clear boundary between nobility and baseness in character and integrity in a clearer and more resonant manner can we claim our victory.

On this point, I truly hope to connect deeply with my listeners' hearts. Our tangled, hesitant thinking in the past was primarily because traces of the Xiaoren still lingered in our hearts, which bred fear. Once we sever those ties, we truly become fearless. Confronting everything we used to dread about Xiaoren, if we steel our resolve and clench our teeth to overcome that fear, we will truly be invincible under heaven. As long as we do not fear Xiaoren, there is virtually nothing in this world we need to fear.

Studying Xiaoren from History to Reality: Awakening Society

Furthermore, there is a concrete step we can take: I urge everyone to earnestly study the problem of Xiaoren from history through to modern reality. Moreover, we must keep discussing this issue extensively—just as I am doing at great length—through various mediums: theater, film, novels, academic essays, and lectures, analyzing it meticulously and presenting it vividly. This will always benefit society's understanding of Xiaoren. Will it be beneficial? Yes, absolutely. Some might claim this won't awaken or deter Xiaoren, but that is not true. I'd like to invoke Molière's comedy The Miser to illustrate this point.

Avarice is actually a smaller and milder defect than being a Xiaoren; everyone possesses a touch of stinginess. To completely eradicate greed and stinginess from human nature is impossible—no one can achieve that. Yet Molière dissected avarice so incisively, pungently, and concretely that after watching the play, whenever people encounter stinginess again, or feel a nascent sprout of greed stirring in their own hearts, they will suddenly realize: 'It feels as though I've seen this before.' At that moment, the laughter from the theater will echo in their ears; that collective laughter reminds them of the baseline of human conscience, prompting them not to isolate themselves outside that laughter. At that moment, one's character quietly ascends a step higher.

Do you see the point I am making by citing The Miser to illustrate the problem of Xiaoren? The same holds true for Xiaoren: never assume that when they watch plays or read essays exposing Xiaoren, they will remain completely unaffected—that is not the case. Human nature is often mixed, containing elements of both Junzi and Xiaoren. When the Xiaoren element surfaces again, they might suddenly recall that an academic paper accurately characterized this very trait, or remember that a TV drama depicted this exact behavior. This sparks analogy and vigilance; with a light smile, they might restrain and rectify their own conduct. Just like Molière writing The Miser, when we produce works examining Xiaoren, it serves as a wake-up call to society—awakening those who carry a greater proportion of Xiaoren tendencies.

Meanwhile, it also provides gentle enlightenment to those with only minor Xiaoren inclinations, which is a wonderful thing. Although this flaw is much milder than full-blown petty malice, laying bare their most common behavior patterns in the open—just as Molière did—will inevitably inspire awakening in people.

A Truly Great Era Is One Where Nobody Bother to Pay Any Mind to Xiaoren

The purpose of studying Xiaoren is to see them clearly and delineate their boundaries, preventing them from appearing disorderly across every corner of our lives. When they appear unpredictably, we find them impossible to ward off. Please note: study should remain strictly within study; try your best never to engage in petty quarrels with them. Arguing only amplifies Xiaoren, whereas studying them renders them weightless. Let me repeat this sentence: arguing amplifies Xiaoren, while studying them renders them weightless.

Many people think, 'I'll just go and argue with the Xiaoren.' In reality, on one hand, you cannot win, because a Xiaoren's logic system is entirely different from that of a Junzi: the things that move you most and bring tears to your eyes leave them completely cold, and their twisted logic is very hard to refute directly. Furthermore, onlookers rarely take a firm stand with your grand, seemingly abstract logic; on the contrary, they often end up feeling sympathy for the clever rhetoric of the Xiaoren. Therefore, dispute only adds weight to them. Moreover, when arguing, a Xiaoren doesn't just use vile language; they readily adopt the high-sounding vocabulary of a Junzi, so by the end of the argument, you are the one made to look like the petty villain. However, once you analyze and articulate the behavioral patterns of humanity's petty persons, you render them weightless—because whenever they act that way, everyone recognizes: 'I've seen this before; I already laughed at this in a TV drama.' And that suffices.

Regarding the definition of a Xiaoren—they are the hardest to define. However, I once read what I consider the finest definition of a Xiaoren in a book by an American scholar. He stated: a truly great era is one where nobody pays any mind to Xiaoren. Let me repeat that sentence: a truly great era is one where nobody pays any mind to Xiaoren. This definition is magnificent. Xiaoren will always exist, but their status is inversely proportional to the weight of the era itself. Autocratic, totalitarian rule often breeds Xiaoren and instills fear in ordinary citizens, thereby emboldening the petty.

If a potent spiritual atmosphere emerges that pushes Xiaoren from the center to the margins of society, from high positions to the lowest rungs, from rulers into superfluous relics, that era has already begun to aspire to greatness. Time does not indefinitely align with the petty. The accelerating pace of our era and an infinitely expansive global perspective gradually render the antics of many Xiaoren increasingly ineffective. Those petty spectacles that caused an uproar just a few years ago are now seen as mere jokes; in particular, those notorious 'great criticism experts' and 'scandal uncoverers'—even their names are already fading from memory.

Yet, our era clearly still has a distance to go before reaching greatness. Creators in the spiritual and cultural spheres are actually numerous, yet they remain besieged by swarms of Xiaoren. Although the Chinese public has grown weary of Xiaoren, their attention—distracted and tugged back and forth by petty controversies for decades—has not yet recovered its capacity to look up at high culture. Consequently, even as Xiaoren are left out in the cold, the most sublime heights of spiritual culture are neglected as well. This is a process of mutual cold-shouldering, but I believe this mutual neglect is merely temporary.

Finally, I must add one point: although Xiaoren as a collective inflict prolonged harm, if you examine their individual lives, most are ultimately pitiful creatures—including the most odious cultural thugs (literary ruffians), who are essentially embittered, depressed failures reared on 'wolf's milk.' In my view, they can still be redeemed. Leaving them out in the cold, setting them aside, and ultimately redeeming them—this is the duty of modern Junzi. At the end of the day, they grew up in an environment devoid of care and love, and therefore they deserve love and care as well. What we despise is their past history of wrongdoing and the collusive structures they once formed in history. Beyond exposing them, giving them the cold shoulder, scorning them, and shelving them, our ultimate goal is to redeem them and care for them. Thank you!
