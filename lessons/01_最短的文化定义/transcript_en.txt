Hello everyone. The first thing today is to repay the debt owed yesterday and announce the definition of culture I have formulated. My definition is this:
Culture is a spiritual value and way of life that becomes a habit, and its final result is a collective personality.
Counting it down, it’s exactly 30 words, the shortest in the world, and it seems it can’t get any shorter. For this shortest definition of culture, I need to explain. Let’s go back to the divorce incident of transnational marriage mentioned earlier. I always believe that such incident touches the deep nature of culture and is related to a person’s settlement and life. It is far more important to culture than a few lines of ancient poems or a few cultural relics.
spiritual values ​​and lifestyle
For example, I know how the first cracks in a transnational marriage arise. The man is Chinese and the woman is American. They were college classmates. They got married in the United States and lived in the United States. The woman is very nice and not harsh at all, but she really doesn’t understand why her husband has to go back to China to visit his grave every Qingming Festival. She thought, the work was so busy, it was not a long vacation, and the journey was so long, why should it be like this every year.
But what the man thinks about is that his parents are old and their days are numbered. With his siblings in full view, I have to come back. This touches on a core spiritual value of the Chinese people, that is, the ethics of family affection; and having to come back at that time every year has become a habitual way of life. This is difficult for women to understand.
After giving this example, let us read my definition again, it will be very easy to understand. Culture is a spiritual value and lifestyle that becomes habitual. It is understandable that the couple divorced due to cultural differences. Starting from this example, think about it, which culture does not involve spiritual values ​​and lifestyles?
Fundamentally speaking, our distant ancestors, whether they lived on the water or hunted for a living, started out just to survive. But when a stable life became a habit, it also became a way of life, and the way is culture. In a certain way of life, people will gradually deal with their relationship with heaven and earth, their family members, and their friends, and then spiritual value will appear. Once spiritual values ​​appear, culture has a backbone.
With the development of history to this day, if you want to ask what is Chinese culture? The clearer answer is that the Chinese people have developed customary spiritual values ​​and lifestyles. For example, Confucian ethics and poetry mainly belong to spiritual values, while the eight major cuisines and traditional Chinese medicine mainly belong to lifestyle. Together they form the best combination of Chinese culture.
There are many regional cultures in the big plate of Chinese culture. For example, what is Shanxi culture and what is Shanghai culture? Similarly, Shanxi people and Shanghai people have also formed customary spiritual values ​​and lifestyles. To further ask, what is the culture of the post-80s and post-90s generations? They also refer to the spiritual values ​​and lifestyles that people of several age groups are accustomed to.
I found that the culture that many cultural scholars cling to is precisely giving up the vitality that culture should have. They often do not strive for spiritual heights when talking to superiors, but look down on basic necessities, food, housing, and transportation to subordinates. They practice small culture and private culture in the pretense of being sophisticated. My shortest definition of culture can help them start over. Go to the spiritual field, enter the ecological land, and create a new culture with new vitality. In my opinion, this is a cultural liberation in its original meaning.
the end result of culture
Having said that, I only stay at the first half of this definition, which is the spiritual values ​​and lifestyle that have become habits. Now for the second half, the ultimate outcome of which is collective personality. The spiritual values ​​and lifestyle that have become habits will, after a long period of precipitation, crystallize into something, which is the collective personality. Personality refers to a person's life style and behavioral norms, and collective personality refers to the common tacit understanding of a group of people in terms of life style and behavioral norms. This kind of mutual tacit understanding does not need to be established, but goes deep into the subconscious mind and becomes an instinct.
This academic depth was first created by the familiar Freud, whose collective unconsciousness has laid a foundation. More importantly, his student and rival Jung clearly stated that the ultimate outcome of all culture is personality. He also said that for all human nations, what is more important is the collective personality of each nation. Jung took Goethe as an example. He said that Faust created by Goethe was a symbol of the collective personality of the German nation. This collective personality is precipitated by culture, so it has already existed. Goethe only wrote it out. So Jung wrote a famous saying: Goethe did not create Faust, but Faust created Goethe.
Having said this, does it sound a bit difficult to you? Then let’s not follow the spirit and return to a more concise narrative. What Jung meant was that the final product of culture is not works, but people, and they are group people. Such group people can look for a symbol, such as Faust. To put it more simply, the final result of Chinese culture is not a large number of books, but a large number of people. In other words, the final achievement of Chinese culture is the collective personality of the Chinese people. According to the above separation, the final result of Shanxi culture is the collective personality of Shanxi people, and the final result of Shanghai culture is the collective personality of Shanghai people.
Jung's statement about collective personality was heard by a Chinese who was six years younger than him, that is Lu Xun. Lu Xun also hoped to find a collective personality for the Chinese people. At that time, he translated it into national character, and he found a symbol of national character, which was Ah Q. In addition to Ah Q, Lu Xun also wrote "Kong Yiji", "Medicine", "New Stories" and other works, all of which are looking for national character, that is, looking for the collective personality of the Chinese people. No modern Chinese writer can match this. He is a modern Chinese writer who truly understands the profound meaning of culture, and may be the only one.
Everyone will definitely ask whether the national character and collective personality of Ah Q and Kong Yiji that Lu Xun found contain great negative qualities. Indeed, this is Lu Xun's severe dissection of Chinese culture. Because according to Jung's theory, Ah Q, Kong Yiji and others are the crystallization of Chinese culture. Lu Xun understood that the way out for Chinese culture was to transform the national character and enhance the collective personality of Ah Q, Kong Yiji and others.
Then we can briefly summarize it: the greatest achievement of Chinese culture is the Chinese people, and the primary task of Chinese culture is to improve the Chinese people. If the Chinese people really improve, Chinese culture will be truly great and its mission will be fulfilled.
ideal model of personality
I included three key words in the world's shortest definition of culture, namely spiritual value, lifestyle, and collective personality. These three keywords are like this. The third keyword - collective personality, is the result of the previous two keywords. In this way, although this definition is short, it is rich in content.
My ideas come from modern times, especially from Jung, a Swiss psychologist. But I must explain right away that Jung’s teachings come from his absorption and digestion of ancient wisdom. For example, in ancient China, many wise men already understood that the symbols and achievements of culture are collective personality. Although they did not express it this way, the meaning was very clear. They were more optimistic than Lu Xun, so they put forward a series of ideals on collective personality very early.
The most typical example is Confucianism. They put forward many ideas on how to govern the country and bring peace to the world, but there is only one idea behind the thousands of ideas, which is that everyone should be gentlemen and not villains. The gentleman and the villain are two opposing collective personalities. Confucius placed them at the starting point and end point of a huge cultural issue, which shows that it echoes the cultural concepts of the modern world.
When I gave speeches at the Library of Congress and Harvard University, I summarized Chinese culture as the way of a gentleman, which was recognized by the audience. The gentleman is indeed an important ideal model of collective personality in Chinese culture. Similar personality models exist in all ethnic groups around the world, but they are very different from each other.
For example, some cultures regard prophets as the model of ideal personality, some cultures regard giants as the model of ideal personality, some cultures regard gentlemen as the model of ideal personality, and some cultures regard warriors as the model of ideal personality. However, the ideal model of personality in Chinese culture is completely different, it is the gentleman. This is the nature and secret of Chinese culture.
Of course, in Chinese culture, Confucianism, which is the mainstream in China, regards the gentleman as the ideal model of personality. Buddhism and Taoism are also dependent on Confucianism. The ideal model of personality in Buddhism is the enlightened person, the awakening of enlightenment; the ideal model of personality in Taoism is the real person, the truth between true and false. Therefore, the gentleman, the enlightened person, and the true person echo each other and complement each other, forming the system of Chinese collective personality, which is also the complete realization system of Chinese culture.

Complementing and coexisting with the ideal of the gentleman, there are the Buddhist ideal of the enlightened man and the Taoist ideal of the real person, which together form the grand connotation of Chinese culture.
